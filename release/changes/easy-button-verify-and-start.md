# Easy button checks first, starts once, and only offers real recordings

While a passage's availability is being checked, the easy button shows no icon and no label: the disc dims and breathes softly (static under reduced motion), its accessible name is "Checking availability" and it is marked busy. It then changes once, to the checked action. A tap while it checks waits and then performs the checked Play or Begin once; a tap that would only advance is dropped. The check has no deadline that guesses: it lasts until the answers arrive. Each check is bounded where it is made: the service-worker requests by their timeouts (10 s to be ready, 45 s to answer), and the launch restore by its own 15 s bound. A returning visit keeps checking until the saved passage has opened and been checked; a restore that has not answered in 15 s ends in the passage-switch fallback, which says the saved passage could not be reached and keeps it for the next launch. When the page's own registration shows that no service worker is coming (blocked, rejected, or its install failed with none active, also when the browser reports the failed worker before it clears it), the check ends at once with that answer instead of after the 10 s ready timeout. If the saved passage cannot open, the default passage is checked again and a tap queued for the saved passage is dropped. A restored term screen that is waiting keeps Continue, which opens the definition, as before. The "This recording is unavailable" notice appears only once the check has ended.

From an accepted Play or Begin until sound, the centre keeps one starting face (the design-book loading row: dimmed, spinner, the label it will have). Repeated taps in that start, and for 800 ms after the last one, never cancel, pause or skip. The centre never becomes Continue or Cancel mid-start; cancel is the labelled control beside it. "Recording ready. Press Play to listen." no longer shows while a requested playback is being carried through.

Play is offered only where a recording can be played or the reviewed catalog admits its preparation. Other screens show no enabled Play and send no preparation request. A saved 'waiting' means the screen was heard, as on Mark 1:1–13 (RECIPE R6.2, K4): navigation, and a move made while availability is still being checked, no longer mark an admitted screen as a silent hold, so it enters waiting only when its own recording ends. Until then Back, reload and reopen offer Begin or Play in the centre. Once heard, the screen shows Continue with Skip beside it, never a Play, whether the recording just ended or the screen comes back by a reload or a reopen.

Validation: new App-boundary tests for the face sequence, slow checks and slow or failed restores (with and without a queued tap), queued taps, tap bursts, carried preparation, R6 offers, the restored term screen, the same heard screen on three arrivals (recording end, reload, reopen) in both presentation modes, and a launch restore that never answers; a unit test for the tap gate; unit tests for the absent-worker answer (including the spec's event order) and for a presentation read with no answer; and a Playwright spec for the load guard (checking, then one verified state, no uncaused change), a returning visit whose restore is held 5.5 s, a returning visit whose restore never answers, the same heard discussion screen on three arrivals, and the six-taps-150-ms hammer. Existing tests that encoded the replaced behaviour were updated in place.

The slot now holds a Play only where a recording exists or can be prepared, whatever the settings; every other screen keeps Skip there, as Scripture screens without audio already did. Screens with a recording keep their Play, its labels and its cancel. Mark 1:1–13's slot therefore shows one Play from first paint, after a reload and after a passage switch, instead of Skip and then Play (K4; J2 runs with automatic guide narration off).
